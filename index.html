How it computes AUC

Each trial’s cursor samples are translated so the start is at (0,0) and scaled so the chosen option lands at (1, 1.5) — with trajectories mirrored so the chosen target is always on the right. Sampling is then re-interpolated to 101 equally-spaced time bins.

AUC is the trapezoidal integral of signed perpendicular distance from the ideal start→target line, with positive values meaning deviation toward the unchosen option. MD is the maximum perpendicular distance.

Why mirror & normalize

Raw pixel trajectories aren’t comparable across screens, monitors, or which side the chosen option fell on. Mirroring puts every chosen target on the same side so AUC is signed consistently; the (0,0)→(1,1.5) frame is the convention from Freeman & Ambady’s MouseTracker.

You will want at least 25–50 trials per condition before treating means as stable.

Caveats

If participants reach a decision before they begin moving, trajectories will be uninformatively straight. The apparatus shows a soft warning when initiation time exceeds 500 ms. For published studies, researchers typically also enforce a deadline (e.g. require movement within 400 ms) and exclude offending trials.
